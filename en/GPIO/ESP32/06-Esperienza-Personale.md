## 9. Personal Experience

> Personal note: the ESP32 is the module that most of all transforms the Flipper Zero from "hacker toy" to "professional tool". Without the ESP32, the Flipper has no WiFi or performant BLE, and this severely limits its use in a real assessment. With the ESP32, it becomes a compact and discreet wireless reconnaissance tool that can replace (for many operations) a laptop with an external WiFi adapter.

> Personal note: my standard setup for a wireless assessment is this: Flipper Zero with Momentum firmware, official ESP32-S2 devboard with Marauder as the main module, an ESP32-WROOM on a breadboard with BLE Killer for when Bluetooth is needed, and a separate ESP32-CAM for surveillance. Three ESP32 modules, each with its own firmware, each with its own role. I alternate between them during the test depending on the phase: Ghost ESP for reconnaissance, Marauder for attacks, Evil Portal for social engineering, Camera for physical security.

> Personal note: I recommend beginners start with a single ESP32-WROOM and Marauder. It's the most versatile tool and the one you learn the most from using. Once you master network scanning and analysis, add Evil Portal for social engineering. The ESP32-CAM is a nice-to-have but is not essential for wireless pentesting. BLE Killer is critical only if the target has BLE IoT devices.

> Personal note: watch out for legality. All WiFi attacks described in this guide (deauth, evil portal, beacon spam) are illegal if performed without explicit authorization. In Italy, the Criminal Code provides severe penalties for unauthorized access to computer systems (art. 615-ter) and for interception of communications (art. 617-quater). Even simple passive scanning in some jurisdictions can be problematic. Always act with a signed contract that specifies exactly what you are authorized to do, on which networks, during which hours, and with which tools. When in doubt, ask for clarification from the client and your legal counsel BEFORE proceeding.

> Personal note: a practical trick for the field -- keep a sheet on the Flipper's microSD with the list of firmware and their respective .bin files ready for flashing with ESP Flasher. When you need to change tools quickly, just look at the sheet, flash the right firmware, and start again. Without this organization, you waste time remembering which .bin corresponds to which tool and at which offset it needs to be flashed. Organization is everything when you're under pressure during an assessment.

> Personal note: the biggest limitation of the Flipper+ESP32 setup is the interface speed. The 128x64 display and 5-button navigation make everything slower compared to a laptop. Don't try to use the Flipper as a laptop replacement -- use it as a complement. The Flipper excels at: quick reconnaissance, targeted attacks, discreet operations where pulling out a laptop would attract attention. For in-depth analysis, cracking, reporting -- use the laptop. The Flipper collects the data, the laptop analyzes it.
